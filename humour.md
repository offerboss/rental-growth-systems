# RGS Humor Style Guide

**Internal guidance only.** This file is not imported into the website, has no public route, and should not be linked from any page. It exists to keep tone consistent across resources, industry pages, solution pages, case studies, and future content written for Rental Growth Systems.

This guide applies to copy written for rental-business owners and operators across the categories RGS serves: dumpster rental, portable toilet, restroom trailer, event rental, and construction/equipment rental companies.

---

## Approved Humor Style

The RGS tone is knowing, helpful, slightly self-aware, confident, and rooted in the day-to-day realities of running a rental business. It reads like it was written by someone who has actually sat in the chair — watched a lead go cold, chased a quote that never got a reply, found a sticky note with a customer's name and no other context. It is not written by someone performing enthusiasm about rental operations from the outside.

The humor should feel earned, not inserted. If a line doesn't come from a real operational truth, it doesn't belong.

**Light understatement**
> "A missed call at 9pm on a Friday is not technically a lost lead. It just feels that way by Monday."

**Relatable operational frustration**
> "Somewhere between the text thread, the shared inbox, and the sticky note on the monitor, a lead stopped being anyone's job."

**Friendly confidence**
> "None of this is complicated. It's just easy to let slip when you're also the one answering the phone, running the route, and doing the quoting."

**Subtle industry observations**
> "Customers don't usually say they're comparing three companies. They just are."

---

## What's Always Off-Limits

- Crude or gross humor
- Insulting rental owners or their employees
- Making customers sound stupid
- Mocking small businesses or the scrappiness required to run one
- Aggressive bro-marketing language
- Exaggerated "guru" language
- Fake bravado or hype-man energy
- Profanity in published website content
- Political or religious humor
- Humor that makes RGS sound unserious
- Jokes that distract from the actual advice

If a line would make a rental company owner feel mocked, talked down to, or like the joke is at their expense, it doesn't run. The humor is always with the operator, never about them.

---

## Approved Scenarios for Light Humor

These are the recurring, real frustrations worth drawing on — always from a place of recognition, never ridicule:

- Missed calls, especially after hours or mid-delivery
- Quote follow-up that quietly never happens
- A sales pipeline that lives in texts, memory, and a notebook
- Too many disconnected tools that don't talk to each other
- Spending more on ads while leads are already leaking out the back
- Asking for a review three weeks after everyone's forgotten the job
- Trying to remember which customer needed which size, unit, or date
- A lead that came in at 11pm and sat until Monday
- A great customer from eight months ago who was never asked to come back

## Tone Calibration

| Too Far | Just Right |
|---|---|
| "Your sales process is a dumpster fire." | "If your sales process lives across texts, spreadsheets, and memory, you're not alone." |
| "You're losing money because your team is lazy about follow-up." | "A quote that goes out and never gets followed up on is a lost job more often than a rejected one." |
| "Stop being cheap and get a real CRM already." | "A CRM only works if somebody actually uses it." |
| "Your website is embarrassing." | "If your quote form asks for more than a name and a phone number, some of your traffic is leaving before you ever see it." |
| "You're basically throwing ad money in the trash." | "Buying more traffic while leads are falling through the cracks is an expensive way to stay busy." |
| "Nobody will rent from you if you can't even answer the phone." | "A missed call doesn't have to be a missed job — it just needs a fast way back to the customer." |
| "Your team is too disorganized to grow." | "Nobody sets out to manage their sales pipeline through text messages and memory. It just sort of happens." |

## Humor in Headlines

**Acceptable**
- "More Leads Won't Fix a Broken Follow-Up System"
- "Your CRM Isn't the Problem. The Way You're Using It Might Be."
- "Before You Spend More on Ads, Fix the Leaks You Already Have"
- "The Rental Lead You Forgot About Is Probably Renting From Someone Else"
- "You Don't Need More Software. You Need Better Systems."

**Not acceptable**

| Headline | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Your Follow-Up Game Is Trash (And So Is Your Spreadsheet)" | Insults the reader directly; crude tone |
| "Stop Being Lazy About Your Leads" | Condescending; blames the owner |
| "The Secret Sauce Every 7-Figure Rental Guru Swears By" | Guru language; fake authority |
| "Your Dumpster Business Stinks (Figuratively. Hopefully.)" | Gimmicky wordplay that undercuts credibility |
| "We Fixed Rental Marketing. You're Welcome." | Fake bravado; arrogant |
| "How to 10X Your Rental Empire Overnight" | Hype-driven, unsupported, guru-adjacent |

## RGS Voice Principles

RGS should read like someone who understands rental businesses from the inside — not a marketing outsider describing them. The voice has:

- **Real familiarity.** It sounds like it has actually dealt with lead flow, ad spend, CRMs, automation, and follow-up, not like it read about them.
- **Operator credibility, not guru energy.** RGS is a knowledgeable operator talking to other operators — not a personality selling a system.
- **Confidence without arrogance.** It can say what works without implying the reader has been doing everything wrong.
- **Practicality over theory.** Every observation should connect to something a rental company owner could recognize or act on this week.

---

## Notes

- Humor is optional. Clarity is not.
- One subtle line lands harder than five jokes in a row.
- If the joke becomes the point, cut it.
- Humor should make the content feel more human — never less credible.
- When in doubt, write it straight.
- The reader should come away feeling understood, not marketed at.
